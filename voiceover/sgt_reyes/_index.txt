Sgt. Reyes - Room 2 - Fire Safety Drill (prevention/intervention/evacuation)
============================================================

001.txt  [NOT_STARTED (intro)]  tags: [friendly, professional]
    Hello, trainee — great to meet you! I'm Sergeant Reyes. Today's lesson has three parts, in this order: prevention first, intervention second, and evacuation last — because preventing a fire beats fighting one, every single time.

002.txt  [NOT_STARTED (intro)]  tags: [calm, instructive]
    Look around this room — a few everyday hazards, just like you'd see in a real building. A frayed cord, a stack of forgotten boxes, an unattended pan. Most fires never have to happen at all if someone catches them early.

003.txt  [NOT_STARTED (intro)]  tags: [encouraging]
    Let's practice that first — spotting a hazard before it becomes a fire, and fixing it with nothing but your own two hands. Follow me!

004.txt  [PREVENTION_DEMO (intro)]  tags: [instructive, upbeat]
    Right-click a glowing hazard to fix it — that's prevention. Work through all three before they get away from you!

005.txt  [PREVENTION_DEMO, hazard 1/3 - archive boxes]  tags: [cautionary, serious]
    See that stack of boxes starting to spark and smoke? Loose paper piled near anything warm is an easy Class A fire waiting to happen.

006.txt  [PREVENTION_DEMO, hazard 1/3 - archive boxes]  tags: [instructive, approving]
    Right-click it now — pretend you're clearing it away from the heat source and stacking it properly. That's it. That's the whole fix.

007.txt  [PREVENTION_DEMO, hazard 2/3 - computer]  tags: [serious, cautionary]
    That computer's sparking — a damaged cord or an overloaded outlet, left plugged in and unwatched.

008.txt  [PREVENTION_DEMO, hazard 2/3 - computer]  tags: [instructive, firm]
    Right-click it — you're unplugging it and setting it aside for a real repair. Never wait on a sparking cord.

009.txt  [PREVENTION_DEMO, hazard 3/3 - grease pan]  tags: [cautionary, serious]
    And that pan — oil left heating with nobody watching it. Kitchen fires start this way more than almost anything else.

010.txt  [PREVENTION_DEMO, hazard 3/3 - grease pan]  tags: [instructive, firm]
    Right-click it — you're pulling it off the heat before it ever catches. Never leave hot oil unattended, even for a minute.

011.txt  [TOOL_SELECTION (intro)]  tags: [upbeat, encouraging]
    Time to gear up — follow the glowing arrow to each extinguisher and I'll walk you through grabbing it.

012.txt  [TOOL_SELECTION, frame 1/3 - ABC red]  tags: [instructive, clear]
    First, the RED ABC extinguisher — see it glowing in its frame? Follow the arrow over.

013.txt  [TOOL_SELECTION, frame 1/3 - ABC red]  tags: [instructive, calm]
    Left-click the frame to pop it loose, then walk over the extinguisher on the floor to pick it up.

014.txt  [TOOL_SELECTION, frame 2/3 - CO2 green]  tags: [instructive, clear]
    Next, the GREEN CO2 extinguisher — for electrical fires. Follow the arrow to its frame.

015.txt  [TOOL_SELECTION, frame 2/3 - CO2 green]  tags: [instructive, calm]
    Same as before: left-click to pop it off the wall, then step onto it to grab it.

016.txt  [TOOL_SELECTION, frame 3/3 - wet chemical yellow]  tags: [instructive, clear]
    Last one — the YELLOW wet chemical extinguisher, for kitchen grease fires. Follow the arrow.

017.txt  [TOOL_SELECTION, frame 3/3 - wet chemical yellow]  tags: [proud, upbeat]
    Left-click the frame, then scoop it up off the floor. That's all three — you're fully equipped!

018.txt  [LIVE_FIRE_DEMO (intro)]  tags: [instructive, encouraging]
    To use an extinguisher: press its number key (1-9) to hold it, aim at the base of the fire, and hold the right mouse button. Match the color to what's burning — you can do it!

019.txt  [LIVE_FIRE_DEMO, fire 1/3 - Class A (boxes)]  tags: [urgent, serious]
    Sometimes prevention doesn't catch it in time — like now. That stack of paper boxes just caught fire! Paper and cardboard are "Class A": ordinary materials.

020.txt  [LIVE_FIRE_DEMO, fire 1/3 - Class A (boxes)]  tags: [urgent, instructive]
    Press your RED ABC extinguisher's number key to hold it, walk close, aim at the base of the fire, and hold the right mouse button. Sweep side to side!

021.txt  [LIVE_FIRE_DEMO, fire 2/3 - Class C (electrical)]  tags: [urgent, alarmed]
    Next — that computer is sparking and now it's alight! That's an electrical fire, "Class C".

022.txt  [LIVE_FIRE_DEMO, fire 2/3 - Class C (electrical)]  tags: [serious, warning]
    Never use water or foam on electrical fires — electricity can travel back to you! Hold your GREEN CO2 extinguisher, get close, and hold the right mouse button to put it out.

023.txt  [LIVE_FIRE_DEMO, fire 3/3 - Class F/K (grease)]  tags: [urgent, alarmed]
    Last one — that pan of cooking oil just caught fire! Grease fires are special: the wrong extinguisher can make them splash and spread.

024.txt  [LIVE_FIRE_DEMO, fire 3/3 - Class F/K (grease)]  tags: [instructive, urgent]
    Hold your YELLOW wet chemical extinguisher, aim at the pan, and hold the right mouse button. It cools the oil and seals it safely. Nice and steady!

025.txt  [LIVE_FIRE_DEMO, stop-drop-roll intro]  tags: [serious, caring]
    One last lesson, and it's an important one: even when you do everything right, sometimes your clothes still catch fire — a stray spark, hot oil, anything. Don't panic when that happens.

026.txt  [LIVE_FIRE_DEMO, stop-drop-roll intro]  tags: [firm, instructive]
    The only way to put yourself out is STOP, DROP, and ROLL: press and hold Shift to drop down low, then press R to roll — keep pressing it until the flames are gone. Nothing else works, so don't waste time trying to swat it out or run for water.

027.txt  [LIVE_FIRE_DEMO, stop-drop-roll intro]  tags: [reassuring, alert]
    Ready? I'm going to light a small, completely safe spark on you now, just like everything else here. The instant you catch fire — Shift, then R!

028.txt  [ALARM_CHECKPOINT]  tags: [proud, warm]
    Well fought!

029.txt  [ALARM_CHECKPOINT]  tags: [serious, instructive]
    But here's what people forget: the moment a fire starts, ring the alarm — before anything else. It warns everyone else in the building, even if you already put the fire out.

030.txt  [ALARM_CHECKPOINT]  tags: [instructive, calm]
    Follow the glowing arrow to the fire alarm and press it. Once it's ringing, come straight back to me.

031.txt  [EVACUATION_BRIEF]  tags: [approving, calm]
    Perfect — alarm's stopped, and everyone's been warned. Now for the very last step, and it's the simplest: once that alarm is ringing, you evacuate. Don't grab your things, don't go back for anything — walk calmly to the nearest exit.

032.txt  [EVACUATION_BRIEF]  tags: [proud, warm]
    Prevention, intervention, evacuation — in that order, every time. You've got all three now. I'm proud of you, trainee!

033.txt  [DONE]  tags: [proud, warm]
    Three fires, three perfect matches — and you even put yourself out safely. I'm proud of you! Follow the glowing arrow to Sgt. Santos for the Earthquake Drill.
